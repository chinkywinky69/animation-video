# Ideas from: "Why You Should Blink Slowly At Your Cat"

## Seed Decode
- **Seed**: "Why You Should Blink Slowly At Your Cat" (topic only; no analytics card was attached). It over-performed, and the creator credits the cute animated scenes, especially the opening hook scene.
- **Niche / lane**: Animals → curiosity explainer, cat-owner sub-lane (warm, cute, practical)
- **Title template**: *Why You Should [small, gentle thing you can do] [At/To] Your Cat*
- **Thumbnail formula**: a big cute cartoon cat face showing the behavior (half-closed "slow blink" eyes) + a small human doing the same thing + ≤4-word caption that names the hidden meaning ("IT MEANS I LOVE YOU"), with one word in red/pink
- **Click devices**: identity hook (it's *your* cat), a secret-language reveal (the cat is "saying" something you didn't know), and a tiny actionable payoff (you can try it tonight)
- **Read**: this one is a *pattern* winner. The title is a template and the payoff is a behavior viewers can try at home, so lean heavily on Tier A. The cute hook scene is half the product, so every idea below has a **hook scene** that animates well: the cat *does something*, then the human *answers it*.
- **Evidence**: cat-communication science is a live topic right now. A Dec 2025 *Ethology* study found cats meow more when men come home, and 2025–26 coverage frames cats as "trying to talk to us." Simon's Cat Logic already covers general body language, so stay specific (one behavior per video) and don't make a generic "cat body language explained" video.

## Tier A — Same pattern

### 1. Why You Should Never Stare At Your Cat
- **Thumbnail**: a big cartoon cat with flattened ears and wide eyes, facing a small human with huge staring eyes. Text: "THAT'S A THREAT" ("THREAT" in red; arrow at the human's eyes)
- **Hook**: "To your cat, that loving gaze you give it every evening looks exactly like a challenge to a fight."
- **Hook scene**: the human gazes adoringly → the camera cuts to the cat's POV, where the human's eyes are giant and glowing like a predator's → the cat slowly backs under the couch.
- **Why it clicks**: a reversal, and the natural sequel to the seed (the stare is the "wrong" version of the slow blink). Viewers who saw the first video click to complete the set.
- **CTR**: 9/10
- **Risk**: overlaps the seed. Present it as the companion piece, not a rehash.

### 2. Why You Should Talk To Your Cat
- **Thumbnail**: a cartoon cat with one ear swiveled toward a small human who is talking (speech bubble with a heart). Text: "IT KNOWS YOUR VOICE" ("VOICE" in red)
- **Hook**: "Your cat can pick your voice out of a crowd of strangers. It just decided not to show it."
- **Hook scene**: three strangers call "here kitty" and the cat doesn't move. You say one word, one ear rotates, and the cat still doesn't look at you.
- **Why it clicks**: a secret-ability reveal plus the funny "aloof but listening" reveal. Studies back it (Saito & Shinozuka 2013 on owner voices; de Mouzon et al. 2022 on cat-directed speech).
- **CTR**: 8/10
- **Risk**: verify the citations in the script.

### 3. Why You Should Say Your Cat's Name
- **Thumbnail**: a cartoon cat whose head is turning, with a name tag floating above it. Text: "IT KNOWS. IT IGNORES." ("IGNORES" in red)
- **Hook**: "Scientists proved your cat knows its name. They also proved it can choose not to come."
- **Hook scene**: the owner calls "Mochi!", one ear flicks, the tail tip twitches once, and the cat keeps loafing.
- **Why it clicks**: a confirmed fact with a comic twist. Saito et al. (2019, *Scientific Reports*) found cats distinguish their own name from other words.
- **CTR**: 8/10
- **Risk**: close to #2. Make only one of them, or merge them into one video.

### 4. Why You Should Ignore Your Cat
- **Thumbnail**: a cartoon human reading a book while a cat climbs onto their head. Text: "THEN IT COMES" ("COMES" in red; arrow at the cat)
- **Hook**: "The fastest way to make a cat love you is to act like you don't care that it exists."
- **Hook scene**: a guest reaches for the cat and the cat flees. The other guest ignores it, and the cat sits on that person's lap.
- **Why it clicks**: a reversal ("the cat person at the party is always the one who hates cats"). It's supported by research on letting cats start the contact (Haywood et al. 2021).
- **CTR**: 9/10
- **Risk**: the title can read as neglect. The script must say "let them start the contact," not "ignore your cat."

### 5. Why You Should Never Pet Your Cat's Belly
- **Thumbnail**: a cartoon cat rolled belly-up with innocent eyes and a small hand reaching in, and a hidden bear-trap outline drawn around the belly. Text: "IT'S A TRAP" ("TRAP" in red)
- **Hook**: "When your cat rolls over and shows you its belly, it isn't asking for a rub. It's paying you a much bigger compliment."
- **Hook scene**: a slow-motion belly reveal with sparkles → the hand goes in → all four paws clamp on.
- **Why it clicks**: a relatable "gotcha" and a reversal with a sweet payoff (showing the belly is a sign of trust). It's very meme-able.
- **CTR**: 8/10
- **Risk**: this topic has been covered before. The "it's a compliment" twist is what makes it different.

### 6. Why You Should Let Your Cat Win
- **Thumbnail**: a cartoon cat proudly holding a feather toy while a small human holds up a trophy. Text: "OR IT GETS ANGRY" ("ANGRY" in red)
- **Hook**: "If your cat attacks your ankles at 3 a.m., it's probably because you never let it finish the hunt."
- **Hook scene**: a laser dot zips around, the cat pounces and finds nothing, the cat stares into the void, and that night it ambushes your ankles.
- **Why it clicks**: it explains a familiar, annoying behavior with a practical fix (always end playtime with a real "catch"), which is the same "you can try it tonight" payoff as the seed.
- **CTR**: 7/10
- **Risk**: the hunt-sequence explanation is common advice from behaviorists. Frame it that way rather than as a specific study.

## Tier B — Same device, new shape

### 7. What Your Cat Means When It Shows You Its Butt
- **Thumbnail**: a cartoon cat with its back to the camera and tail straight up, looking back over its shoulder with a smile, and a small horrified human. Text: "IT'S A COMPLIMENT" ("COMPLIMENT" in pink)
- **Hook**: "Every time your cat sticks its butt in your face, it's saying hello the polite way cats do."
- **Hook scene**: a laptop work call, the cat walks across the keyboard, turns, tail up, and the coworkers on screen gasp.
- **Why it clicks**: it's funny, everyone has seen it happen, and the reveal is sweet (tail-up is a friendly greeting, and cats sniff each other there to say hello).
- **CTR**: 9/10
- **Risk**: keep the thumbnail cute rather than crude, so it stays advertiser-friendly.

### 8. Why Your Cat Only Meows At You
- **Thumbnail**: two cartoon cats sitting together in silence, one of them meowing at a small human with a big speech bubble. Text: "NOT TO OTHER CATS" ("NOT" in red)
- **Hook**: "Adult cats almost never meow at each other. That sound is a language they invented just for you."
- **Hook scene**: the cats sit in total silence like a library → the human walks in → "MEOW."
- **Why it clicks**: a hidden-system reveal ("a language just for humans"). It's well supported: adult cats rarely meow to each other, and meowing is aimed at people.
- **CTR**: 9/10
- **Risk**: low. This is the cleanest and strongest fact in the batch.

### 9. Why Your Cat Headbutts You
- **Thumbnail**: a cartoon cat pressing its forehead into a human's cheek, with a little "stamp" mark left behind. Text: "YOU'RE CLAIMED" ("CLAIMED" in red)
- **Hook**: "When your cat bonks its head into you, it's leaving a label on you that only other cats can read."
- **Hook scene**: the cat bonks the human, a glowing "PROPERTY OF MOCHI" stamp appears that only the neighbor's cat can see, and the neighbor's cat backs off.
- **Why it clicks**: it shows an invisible system (scent marking, also called bunting) and works as both love and ownership.
- **CTR**: 7/10
- **Risk**: the "ownership" framing needs a soft landing: it's affection plus a shared group scent.

### 10. Your Cat Thinks You're a Terrible Hunter
- **Thumbnail**: a cartoon cat dropping a toy mouse at a small human's feet with a pitying look. Text: "IT'S TEACHING YOU" ("TEACHING" in red)
- **Hook**: "That dead mouse on your doorstep may not be a gift. Your cat might be worried you'll starve."
- **Hook scene**: the cat watches the human fail to catch a fly five times, sighs, and goes outside.
- **Why it clicks**: a human frame (the cat as a disappointed parent or teacher) and a funny reversal of the "it's a gift" belief.
- **CTR**: 8/10
- **Risk**: the "teaching" idea is a popular theory, not settled science. Present several explanations (instinct, safe storage, teaching) to stay honest.

## Tier C — Wildcards

### 11. Your Cat Thinks You're a Giant, Clumsy Cat
- **Thumbnail**: a cartoon cat looking up at a huge human drawn with cat ears and whiskers, who is tripping over a rug. Text: "A BIG DUMB CAT" ("DUMB" in red)
- **Hook**: "Dogs know you're a different species. Your cat? It probably thinks you're just a very large, very clumsy cat."
- **Hook scene**: the screen switches to "cat vision" and the human turns into a giant cat-human that knocks things over, while the real cat watches, embarrassed.
- **Why it clicks**: the biggest anthropomorphism swing in the batch, and it's built for cute scenes. It comes from John Bradshaw's *Cat Sense*: cats treat us much like they treat other cats.
- **CTR**: 8/10 (could be the next outlier)
- **Risk**: it's an interpretation, not a proven fact. Say "scientists think" in the script.

### 12. What Your Cat Does All Day While You're At Work
- **Thumbnail**: a cartoon cat at a window with a clock reading 9:00 → 5:00 and a tiny "missed you" heart. Text: "IT WAITS AT 4:59" ("4:59" in red)
- **Hook**: "The moment your front door closes, your cat's real day begins, and it knows exactly when you'll be back."
- **Hook scene**: time-lapse animation of sleeping, a window patrol, sleeping again, and the cat sitting by the door one minute before the key turns.
- **Why it clicks**: it compresses a whole day into a few minutes and lets viewers see something they never get to watch. It gives you the most scenes to build.
- **CTR**: 7/10
- **Risk**: the facts are thin. Anchor it to real numbers (cats sleep 12–16 h/day; studies on cats noticing when owners are gone) and avoid made-up specifics.

## If I had to pick three
1. **Why Your Cat Only Meows At You**: the strongest true fact, a "language made for you" reveal, and the closest emotional match to the slow-blink video.
2. **Why You Should Never Stare At Your Cat**: the direct sequel. People who watched the seed click it to finish the set, and the cat-POV hook scene is very visual.
3. **What Your Cat Means When It Shows You Its Butt**: the highest curiosity and the funniest hook scene. It broadens the series from sweet to sweet-and-funny.

Series note: an "Every Secret Message Your Cat Sends You" compilation (slow blink, tail up, headbutt, meow, belly) works well as a capstone *after* 4–5 of these are out. Don't make it first, or it uses up the material for the single-topic videos.
